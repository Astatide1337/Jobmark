Many goal-setting routines fail because they stay too broad to guide a real week.

Writing a goal down is a good start, but you also need a clear result, a next step, and a way to check what changed.

## The three rules

**Rule 1: Make goals clear**

- Bad: "Get better at communication."
- Good: "Send a weekly update to the people I work with, with decisions, risks, and open items."

The first is too broad to check. The second is clear enough to measure.

**Rule 2: Make goals hard but possible**

- Bad: "Complete all assigned tasks."
- Good: "Reduce average ticket resolution time from 2 days to 1.5 days."

Easy goals may not push you. Hard goals can help, but they still need to be possible.

**Rule 3: Check your progress**

Set a goal. Check it. Change it when needed.

If you do not check your progress, you will not know if the goal is working. Change the plan when needed.

## How to apply this weekly

Every Sunday (or whenever you plan), write 2-3 goals for the week.

Each goal needs:

- What specifically will be different by Friday?
- How will you know you hit it?
- What number or result will you track?

Example:

- Goal: "Reduce code review backlog."
- Measure: "PRs older than 2 days drop from 12 to 8."
- Check: Check daily.

## The one thing

You can have three goals. But pick one as the main thing.

If everything matters, nothing does. Choose one thing to do first.

Put your energy there first. Then, if you have time, work on the others.

## When goals feel wrong

Sometimes a goal becomes irrelevant mid-week. Priorities shift. Context changes.

That's fine. Update the goal. Acknowledging you were wrong is not failure. Chasing a bad goal is.

## Use weekly goals to plan longer work

Weekly goals help with monthly goals. Monthly goals help with quarterly plans.

Do not worry about the names. Make sure each week has a clear direction.

A vague plan to "try hard" is not enough. A clear plan and regular checks help.

Try this for six weeks. Track your progress. You'll see the difference.
