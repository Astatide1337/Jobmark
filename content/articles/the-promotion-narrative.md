People can miss your progress when they only see separate tasks.

A promotion case puts the work in order. It shows what you did, what changed, and what you can do next.

## What it is

A promotion case is more than a list of tasks. It explains the change over time.

For example:

"I started by doing my work well. Then I learned to lead projects. Then I made bigger decisions. Now I am ready to own a whole area."

That is the part people need to understand.

## Why it matters

When promotion time comes, people ask: "Is this person ready for more responsibility?"

Make the answer easy to see. Show the work and the results.

## Building it over time

Do not write the case only once a year. Add to it as you work.

Every quarter, ask:

- What's the one big thing I delivered?
- What did I learn?
- How am I growing?

Save those answers. They become your notes for the next review.

## The format

A good promotion case has three parts:

**1. Where I was**

Brief. What was your starting point? What were you responsible for?

**2. Where I am now**

What have you delivered? What's changed because of your work? What have you learned?

**3. Where I'm going**

What do you want next? What are you preparing for? What would more responsibility look like?

Use this structure each time you update it.

## Updating it

Do not wait for review time to write your case.

Every quarter, spend 30 minutes. Write the current version.

Look for a clear change over time. Does the case make sense?

If it doesn't, figure out what's missing. Then go fill that gap.

## Using it

When you have a promotion conversation, you don't start from zero.

You say: "Here's what I've been working on. Here's what I've delivered. Here's where I'm headed."

You've already done the work. You're just sharing it.

## The alternative

Without a clear case, people have to piece together separate notes.

Some will get it. Many won't.

You know your work best. Write it down so other people can see it too.
