Networking can feel forced when every message feels like a trade. A small routine is easier to keep.

But relationships matter. Opportunities come from people who know you. Trust is built before it's needed.

You do not need to send many messages. Pick a few people and stay in touch.

## The truth about relationships

You do not need a huge network. You need a few strong relationships.

Having three people who know your work and will speak well of you is worth more than having three hundred LinkedIn connections.

Focus on a few real relationships.

## A small routine

**Monthly:**

- Reconnect with one person you haven't talked to in a while.
- Send them something that may help: an article, a resource, or a short note.

That's it. One person a month. Twelve conversations a year.

This takes maybe 15 minutes a month.

## What to send

Not "Hey, how are you?" That's a question. It demands a response. It creates obligation.

Send something that may help:

- "Saw this and thought of you" + article
- "Remember when you mentioned you were working on X? Found this and thought it might help."
- "Saw news about your company/team. Interesting. Let's catch up sometime."

This gives the other person a reason to reply without asking for anything.

## Before you need help

Most people only send a message when they need something. Stay in touch before then.

## When you need help

Here is what this routine gives you:

Six months from now, you need something. A referral. A piece of advice. An introduction.

You send a message to one of these people. They already know you and your work.

Your request is easier because the relationship is already there.

Without regular contact, the message can feel unexpected. That is why networking feels hard.

## The network you already have

Don't forget the people you already know.

- Old coworkers
- Classmates
- Friends from before your career
- People you've worked with briefly

These are easier to maintain than new connections. They already know you. Start there.

## The smallest version

If you're busy, do this:

Once a month, send one helpful note to one person.

That is the whole routine.

Over time, you will have a few people who know your work and may be able to help when you ask.
